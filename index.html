Research

I am an independent researcher and AI/ML engineer working at the intersection of Low-resource NLP, speech system evaluation and benchmarking, and AI safety. My work focuses on building and rigorously evaluating AI systems in African linguistic and cultural contexts, identifying where they fail, why they fail, and what it takes to make them genuinely safe and useful for the communities they claim to serve.

My research spans low-resource language data curation, evaluation and benchmarking of multilingual NLP and speech systems, and the development of methods for reliable AI in African linguistic contexts. I study how errors propagate across cascaded systems, from machine translation and text-to-speech to automatic speech recognition and language models, and develop evaluation methods that capture failures overlooked by conventional benchmarks. My work also explores adaptation and self-training approaches for speech models in code-switched and data-scarce settings. Most recently, my team developed a novel metric for evaluating ASR systems in low-resource African languages, addressing limitations in existing evaluation practices; this work was presented and recognized with an award at the Deep Learning Indaba 2026.

I am increasingly drawn to AI safety and alignment, particularly the question of what safety means when systems are deployed in multilingual, culturally grounded, high-stakes contexts — and how evaluation infrastructure can be the foundation for making AI systems more reliable and equitable across Africa.

Selected Publications

Deep Learning Indaba 2026 — RIAD Poster

Tone on a Budget: A Reference-Free Metric for Lexical Tone in Massively-Multilingual TTS

Daudu, M., Felix, F.†, & Bezaleel, H.-J.† — ML Collective (†equal contribution)

Introduces tone_i2, the first automated, reference-free tone-error metric for synthesised African speech — oracle-validated to catch tonal degradations CER is structurally blind to. Applied to Yorùbá TTS: near-native tone zero-shot, with fine-tuning buying intelligibility (CER 5.6%→1.8%), not tone.

WiML @ ICML 2026 — Poster

Why Large Language Models Fail for Hausa Educational Content: Cascading Errors From Translation to Speech to Comprehension

Bezaleel, H.-J., Jim, P., & Daudu, M. — ML Collective, UniCBT

Evaluates end-to-end LLM pipelines on HausaPQ-Speech (4,703 WASSCE questions), demonstrating that MT omission, ASR tonal collapse, and LLM hallucination compound to keep QA accuracy below 30%. Concludes that fully automated systems are not yet viable for Hausa educational deployment.

Community Impact

My commitment to democratising AI research extends beyond technical contributions to building infrastructure for inclusive participation. I founded the Data Scientist Network (DSN) EKSU — the first university-wide data science community at Ekiti State University — growing it to over 1,500 members and mentoring 300+ students in machine learning, NLP, and research fundamentals.

As a Microsoft Learn Student Ambassador, I trained 650+ ambassadors globally, launched EKSU's tech community, and organised Microsoft DevConnect 2025, a flagship 500+ attendee event. I have spoken at over a dozen regional conferences including DevFest Ado, DataFest Africa, IndabaX, and WomenTechMakers on AI, data literacy, and digital empowerment.

In 2024, I led an AI Literacy initiative in Ado-Ekiti, delivering GenAI training to 50+ teachers and awareness sessions to 7,000+ rural secondary school students — one of the largest grassroots AI literacy efforts in Southwest Nigeria.

Background

I hold a B.Sc. in Statistics from Ekiti State University (2024), where coursework in probability theory, linear algebra, and stochastic processes laid the quantitative foundation for my ML research. My path into independent research was community-taught: early mentorship through Data Science Nigeria, deep involvement with Google Developer Student Clubs, and consistent participation in Deep Learning Indaba shaped my research sensibilities before any formal affiliation.

Three years of production ML work — spanning low-resource ASR/TTS, neural machine translation, fintech classification, and MLOps — now run alongside active research. I believe the most important AI problems for the African continent will be solved by researchers who are embedded in those communities, not just studying them from outside.

Outside research, I enjoy playing chess, cooking, fashion illustration, reading, and listening to podcasts. I'm always open to conversations that connect technical work to broader social impact.
